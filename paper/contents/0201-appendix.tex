\section{Prompt templates}
\label{app:prompts}

\subsection{Sentence granularity}

The instruction below is \gls{bless} Prompt~2 \parencite[13294]{kew-etal-2023-bless} reproduced verbatim, with the single parameterisation described in \cref{sec:method-three}: the paper's hard-coded noun phrase \emph{non-native speakers of English} becomes the slot \texttt{\{target\_audience\}}.
Rendering the slot with its default value therefore returns the published prompt character for character, which a unit test asserts.

\begin{promptbox}
	Please rewrite the following complex sentence in order to make it easier to understand by \{target\_audience\}. You can do so by replacing complex words with simpler synonyms (i.e. paraphrasing), deleting unimportant information (i.e. compression), and/or splitting a long complex sentence into several simpler ones. The final simplified sentence needs to be grammatical, fluent, and retain the main ideas of its original counterpart without altering its meaning.
\end{promptbox}

The slot accepts only a closed set of values rather than free text, so that evaluation runs stay reproducible, and the slot cannot carry injected instructions alongside the page content.
The set has five values:

\begin{promptbox}
	non-native speakers of English \hfill (default) \\
	children aged 8 to 12 \\
	adult readers with low literacy skills \\
	readers with cognitive disabilities \\
	a general adult audience with no specialist knowledge
\end{promptbox}

The rendered prompt places the instruction first, then the three demonstrations of \cref{app:demos} as \texttt{Complex:}/\texttt{Simple:} pairs, then the input and a dangling \texttt{Simple:} prefix for the model to continue from, with a blank line between blocks:

\begin{promptbox}
	<instruction> \\
	\\
	Complex: <demonstration 1 source> \\
	Simple: <demonstration 1 reference> \\
	\\
	Complex: <demonstration 2 source> \\
	Simple: <demonstration 2 reference> \\
	\\
	Complex: <demonstration 3 source> \\
	Simple: <demonstration 3 reference> \\
	\\
	Complex: <input sentence> \\
	Simple:
\end{promptbox}

\subsection{Document granularity}

The document template has no published source.
It adapts the sentence template in the three ways recorded in \cref{sec:method-three}, with the frame \texttt{Text:}/\texttt{Simplified text:} in place of \texttt{Complex:}/\texttt{Simple:}, and carries no demonstrations by default.

\begin{promptbox}
	Please rewrite the following text in order to make it easier to understand by \{target\_audience\}. You can do so by replacing complex words with simpler synonyms (i.e. paraphrasing), deleting unimportant information (i.e. compression), splitting long complex sentences into several simpler ones, and merging or reordering sentences where that makes the whole text clearer. The result needs to be grammatical, fluent, and retain the main ideas of the original without altering its meaning. Return only the rewritten text as continuous prose -{}- no commentary, no headings, no bullet points, and no introduction such as "Here is the simplified text".
\end{promptbox}

\section{Hand-labelling codebook}
\label{app:codebook}

The \num{130} items behind \cref{fig:verdicts} were sampled and labelled under the scheme below.
The sample is stratified by page rather than drawn from the pool, three items per page per cut, because the question is how the filters behave across page types.
A flat draw from the skipped pool would be mostly Wikipedia and MDN.
Each item's rank is the SHA-256 of its own text, so the sample is deterministic without a random seed and each label stays attached to its item.

The skipped side is restricted to items the filters actually decided about, meaning prose-length text of at least eight words inside the page's own content area.
Section headings are excluded because the document cut skips those by design.
A sample of skipped navigation labels would be labelled correct almost everywhere and measure nothing.
The unchanged side draws from every unit the model was given and handed back unaltered.
